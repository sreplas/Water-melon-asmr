Inside the experiment

The body. The slice is a volumetric lattice of — tetrahedra. Each one carries a pair of XPBD constraints from a stable Neo-Hookean material: one resists changes of shape, the other holds its volume, and a last pass never lets an element turn inside out. The rind is 2.4× firmer than the flesh.

The clock. Physics advances on a fixed 120 Hz step split into 8 substeps, capped per frame so a slow frame becomes slow motion instead of an explosion. Damping acts only on deformation — the slice keeps its momentum, but the wobble fades. The floor adds friction and a soft, low bounce.

The surface. A WebGPU compute pass embeds a smooth, rounded —-vertex skin, — seeds and a few air bubbles in the deforming lattice every frame and rebuilds normals, so everything moves and turns with the flesh.

The tools. A cut or a scoop really changes the topology: the knife's plane or the spoon's swept bowl is carried into the slice's rest space, each piece it touches is re-tetrahedralised from its new shape, split into connected bodies and handed back to the solver without a pause. The mallet is a kinematic capsule swept smoothly through the substeps, so the jelly compresses, bulges and rebounds on its own. Every new face takes its colour from the same rest-space material field, so the flesh, the pale layer and the rind — and the seeds, which follow their flesh — sit exactly where they would.

The light. A back-face pass measures how much jelly each pixel looks through. The front pass refracts the studio behind it, absorbs light by thickness (Beer–Lambert), adds internal scattering, Fresnel reflection and glossy highlights, and lets thin edges glow. Contact shadows come from a height map and a soft shadow map.

This specimen needs WebGPU.

Melon Jelly is simulated and rendered live with native WebGPU and WGSL shaders. There is deliberately no WebGL, video or still-image stand-in, so nothing is shown here instead.

Try a current Chrome or Edge on desktop, Chrome on Android, or Safari 26 or later. Firefox ships WebGPU on Windows from version 141. If your browser supports WebGPU, check that hardware acceleration is switched on and reload.
